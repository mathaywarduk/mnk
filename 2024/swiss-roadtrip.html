Rudi uses his passport

Switzerland

June 2024

Last year we’d decided not to travel to Switzerland as it was just too much hassle to get Rudi’s health certificate at short notice, but this year we made it work.

We drove down to Folkestone and took Le Shuttle over to France for a night before making our way to Switzerland and into the mountains as soon as we could.

Our first two nights in Switzerland, we stayed at Camping Rutti, where we explored the hills behind and met the local cows.

Eiger view

Lauterbrunnen, the nearest town to Camping Rutti was a tourist trap and Kerry took pleasure in honking the horn at a selfie taker. Just down the road and a little less budy was Grindelwald, where we stayed next at the Eiger View Alpine Lodge. It’s name was spot on.

From there, we took the cable car up to go for a walk. Great views on the way up, but when we got there the trail was closed. So, instead we drove over to Kanderstag and walked up to Oeschinensee.

Memorable drive to Camping Viva

One of our favourite campsites was Camping Viva, although getting there was a bit more difficult than expected. After a wrong turn, we drove 17km through a tunnel and then over a mountain pass, with cobbled roads and hairpin bends. Wonderful for Mat, terrifying for Kerry.

The next day we took a walk up the river (and a little dip), stopped in the town for a couple of beers and later had a barbeque in the park.

Shattered by this point, we found a hotel and crashed. Turns out it had good beer, wine and food, which was a bonus the next day!

Staying on the mountain

We wanted to stay in a mountain hut, but the weather meant our planned trip was cancelled. Last minute we booked Berggasthaus Ebenalp as it was near the Schäfler Ridge which was on the list to visit.

It was such a cute place and we watched Switzerland in the Euros on their big screen while Rudi made friends with a German man with a sausage in his bag.

The next day we walked along to the Schäfler Ridge and down to Seealpsee and a great lemony beer.

Saignelegier and Arolla

Next, we stayed at two very different campsites. One, in the trees and with campfires, we watched England in the Euros with a couple of beers and the other felt the most ‘alpine’ of the campsites we stayed at.

From the second, we walked up the hill to Lac Blu, avoiding the electric fences where we could.

Relaxed in Zermatt

Finally we pushed the boat out and booked a stay at Cervo, a spa hotel in Zermatt.

The place smelt incredible (Mat is still searching for the same scent), it had great views of the Matterhorn and an incredible secret breakfast room.

We took the furnicular up to Sunegga, walked around (some of) the five lakes and down to Chez Vrony for a bougie lunch with a view.
